This data set is competition assay data. I am competing different mutant strains of pseudomonas fluorescens against the WT to see if any mutants are more fit based on CFU colony counts. The mutant strain is tagged with kanomycin resistance (KM) and the WT is tagged with streptomycin resistance so we can see the relative fitness between the two strains when grown on plates supplemented with KM or SM. The number of CFU on day 4 compared to the seeded baseline is used to calculate the relative fitness between 3 technical replicates. The malthusian, the comparison of CFU on day 0 compared to day 4 is calculated by ln(CFU day4/CFU Day0).

My general question is are any of the supermucoid mutants (SM) more fit than the WT.

Relevant variables: relative fitness: The Malthusian parameter for each strain is computed as ln(CFU Day 4 / CFU Day 0), and relative fitness is the ratio of these values between the two co-competing strains.

fitness_long <- relative_fitness %>%
  select(competition = Competition, rep_A = `Rep 1`, rep_B = `Rep 2`, rep_C = `Rep 3`) %>%
  pivot_longer(
    cols      = starts_with("rep_"),
    names_to  = "replicate",
    values_to = "relative_fitness"
  ) %>%
  mutate(
    competition = factor(competition, levels = c(
      "WT Km vs. WT Sm",
      "∆E Km vs. WT Sm",
      "SM Km vs. WT Sm",
      "SM* Km vs. WT Sm",
      "SM 3832vs. WT Sm"
    ))
  ) %>%
  filter(!is.na(relative_fitness))

Hypotheses Null hypothesis (H0): Mutations do not have a fitness advantage when competed against the WT strain in P fluorescens. Alternative hypothesis (H1): Mutations do have a fitness advantage when competed against the wild type.

Statistic test. I am using an anova because I am comparing the relative fitness between 5 different groups. It can test all 5 groups simultaneously rather than doing multiple T-tests. A Tukey HSD can tell which pairs/combinations of groups are significant.
